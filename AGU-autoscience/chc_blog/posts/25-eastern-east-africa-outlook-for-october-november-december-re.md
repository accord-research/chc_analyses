# Eastern East Africa outlook for October-November-December remains pessimistic in August, but drought concerns diminish slightly

Chris Funk, Laura Harrison, Gideon Galu, Diriba Korecha, Chris Shitote, Shraddhanand Shukla, and Greg Husak

This brief post updates our outlook for eastern East Africa, using the**I**ndo-**W**arm Pool**H**eating**G**radient (IWHG) modeling approach and new sea surface temperature forecasts from early August.

The IWHG modeling approach translates seasonal sea surface temperature (SST) forecasts for three regions in the tropical Indian and Pacific Oceans into a metric that describes the intensity of the east-west IWHG. This contrasts atmospheric heating over the western Indian Ocean with heating over the eastern Indian Ocean and western Pacific Ocean. Strong positive IWHG values are associated with above-normal or extreme October-November-December (OND) Eastern Horn (eHorn) rains. Strong negative IWHG values are associated with below-normal precipitation during the OND season. The three SST factors used to estimate the IWHG are i) the Indian Ocean Dipole (IOD), ii) equatorial west Pacific SST anomalies, and iii) Niño3.4 SST anomalies in the equatorial eastern Pacific. These values are expressed as normalized anomalies from a 1991-2020 baseline. Please see the longer July blog post[(link](https://blog.chc.ucsb.edu/?p=1418)) at post for details on IWHG modeling.

For this outlook, we use OND SST forecasts from seven models from the North American Multi-Model Ensemble (NMME). These model forecasts are shown below (Figure 1).

The boxes over the Pacific Ocean in Figure 1 identify the equatorial west Pacific and the Niño3.4 region. While the specific values of the anomalies differ for each model, there is general agreement on overall sign and pattern of the anomalies across all models. The equatorial west Pacific SST forecasts range from +0.4℃  to +0.8℃, with an average forecast of +0.6℃. The Niño3.4 SST forecasts range from -0.5℃ to -1.4℃, with an average of -0.9℃. These results suggest that La Niña conditions are likely, and the impact of cool east Pacific SST anomalies will be exacerbated by the influence of an abnormally warm West Pacific.  However, the NMME SST forecasts for these regions became slightly more moderate in August, compared to forecasts made in July. The July west Pacific and Niño3.4 forecasts were +0.7℃ and -1.2℃.

The boxes over the Indian Ocean indicate the west and east boxes that correspond to the IOD. Here we see substantial differences between the different models. Forecasting Indian Ocean SSTs is more difficult than forecasting Pacific temperatures. The IOD forecasts range from slightly positive (+0.5℃ in the GOES-S2S model) to moderately negative (-0.6℃ in GFDL-SPEAR). The average IOD forecast by August-initialized models is -0.1℃. Between July and August,  the mean NMME IOD forecast shifted closer to average, from -0.4℃ to -0.1℃. This implies a shift from expecting aweak to moderate strength negativeIOD to an expectation of weak negative or neutral IOD conditions.

Translating the SST assumptions into IWHG values (see last month’s post for methods), results in an IWHG forecast of -189 Wm-2. Using by-pixel IWHG regressions we can derive the OND rainfall percent-of-average forecasts shown in Figure 2 (left). This outlook is less pessimistic than last month’s IWHG forecast map (central panel), based on an IWHG forecast of -256 Wm-2. Nevertheless, eastern Kenya, southern Ethiopia and much of Somalia still appear likely to face below-normal rains. Our latest IWHG forecast (Figure 3), suggests that there is a good chance (67%) of the IWHG being in a moderate drought category (between -350 and -100 Wm-2), a 23% chance of the gradient being in non-drought category (> -100 Wm-2), and a 10% chance of being in severe drought category, with values less than -350 Wm-2. Such an outlook appears consistent with preliminary forecasts from[ICPAC](https://www.icpac.net/)and the[Kenya Meteorological Department](https://meteo.go.ke/).Both agencies will issue their latest official forecasts next week.As a recent multi-agency[alert](https://reliefweb.int/report/ethiopia/preparedness-planning-needed-potential-drought-eastern-horn-africa-during-october-december-rainy-season)highlighted, across a host of agencies, there is broad consensus that:**

- *With varying degrees of certainty, climate models predict a transition to La Niña conditions during the second half of 2024, which would likely bring below-average rainfall to the eastern Horn of Africa. The greatest impacts are expected in central and southern Somalia, southern Ethiopia, and the arid and semi-arid lands of Kenya.*

- *Should below-average rains materialize, crop failures, deteriorating pastoral conditions, water shortages, atypical livestock movements, increased disease outbreaks, and heightened food insecurity and malnutrition are likely.*

- *Humanitarian partners should engage with local and national governments to support their contingency planning, the implementation of preparedness activities and the identification of anticipatory actions to mitigate the impacts of potential below-average rains.***

Figure 3 shows an updated IWHG-eHorn Standardized Precipitation Index scatter plot. This can be contrasted with a similar Figure 4B from last month ([here](https://blog.chc.ucsb.edu/?p=1418)). While the overall probability of dryness has decreased, the outlook still indicates that below-normal rainfall is the most-likely outcome. Extremely strong negative IWHG values, associated with severe drought (Figure 2-left) appear unlikely, given the August NMME OND 2024 SST forecasts. Concerns for dry OND outcomes, however, persist.

It is interesting to compare the August 2024 OND NMME precipitation forecasts with August NMME forecasts for 2020, 2021 and 2024. Overall, the 2024 forecast appears the most similar to 2020 – a period during which we saw a transition from neutral Indo-Pacific climate conditions into a moderate La Niña. The observed IWHG value in 2020 (-178 Wm-2) resembles the current NMME-based forecast of -189 Wm-2. In comparison, the observed 2021 and 2022 IWHG values (-268 and -328 Wm-2) indicated more dangerous drought conditions.

In summary, the overall conditions we face appear broadly similar to 2020. We have yet to see the establishment of La Niña or negative IOD conditions, though current 15-day rainfall forecasts ([here](https://reliefweb.int/report/ethiopia/preparedness-planning-needed-potential-drought-eastern-horn-africa-during-october-december-rainy-season)) do anticipate heavy rainfall over the equatorial eastern Indian Ocean and western Pacific. Between the July and August forecasts, there has been a slight movement in the IWHG outlook towards more neutral conditions, but this could easily change over the next few months, especially if La Niña or negative IOD conditions emerge. It is also worth remembering that ‘normal’ in many arid and semi-arid regions of East Africa means marginal rainfall totals within a very warm environment with high atmospheric water demand.  In many of these regions, seasonal rains of less than 75% of normal may be insufficient to support successful crops, rangelands, and water availability outcomes.
