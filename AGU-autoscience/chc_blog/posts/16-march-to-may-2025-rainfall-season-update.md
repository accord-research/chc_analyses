# March to May 2025 Rainfall Season Update

Laura Harrison, Chris Funk, Daniella Alaso, and Will Turner

Given the severe food insecurity in parts of East Africa, it is heartening to see beneficial rains in areas of Kenya and southern Somalia where there was concern about the March to May (MAM) 2025 season. Despite strong indications from sea surface observations and climate model forecasts for a below-normal MAM 2025 rainfall season in eastern East Africa, we experienced a rapidly waning La Niña during the past two months. Regional factors and intraseasonal atmospheric influences increased onshore moisture transports and rainfall in equatorial and southern areas of East Africa. This blog describes the latest conditions and forecasts.

**A wetter-than-expected beginning to the March to May 2025 rainfall season**

The East Africa’s March to May rain season was established across many areas during March (Figure 1), with a timely or[earlier-than-normal onset](https://data.chc.ucsb.edu/people/will/Season_WRSI_Monitoring/East_Africa/MarNov_LongRains/Sorghum/with_Forecast/SubX/Archive/LikelySOSAnomaly_2025_d10.png)observed or forecasted in most areas. The passage of the intraseasonal Madden Julian Oscillation in early-mid March contributed to good rainfall performance.[March rainfall](https://data.chc.ucsb.edu/products/Season_Monitor/africa_east/mar_to_sep/pngs/archive/Anomaly_06PentAccum_2025_p18.png)totals were above average across most of Kenya, southwestern Ethiopia, and southern Somalia. Areas with later-than-normal onset dates include western Kenya, where a dry February delayed onset by several weeks but rains have since been above average, and in central and northern Ethiopia where Belg season rains have been below average.

**Indian Ocean conditions are supporting ample rains in April**

Recent and forecast conditions in the Indian Ocean indicate that beneficial rains in eastern equatorial and southern East Africa will continue at least through mid-April.

Low-level southerly and southeasterly winds that bring moisture from the southern tropical Indian Ocean were stronger than normal during early April (Figure 2a-b). Atmospheric moisture content was higher than usual, especially in northeastern Tanzania, central and eastern Kenya, and southwestern Somalia, also according to NCEP/NCAR Reanalysis data (Figure 2c). These low-level winds have been moving across areas of the southern tropical Indian Ocean that are humid and very warm, with sea surface temperatures between 28.5 and 30 °C (Figure 2d). These are bringing rainfall-supportive atmospheric conditions into eastern equatorial and southern East Africa.

According to preliminary outlooks through mid-April, additional areas in southern and central Somalia will likely have above-normal seasonal rainfall totals. March rainfall surplus areas will also increase and expand in Kenya, southern Ethiopia, Uganda, Tanzania, and South Sudan, if the unbiased GEFS (CHIRPS-GEFS) forecast rainfall amounts occur (Figure 3). Below-average seasonal rains may continue in Ethiopia’s central and northern Belg regions.

The GEFS week 1 and week 2 forecasts indicate that the enhanced low-level southerly and southeasterly winds will continue during these weeks (Figure 4a). Chances of above-normal rainfall are moderately high (>50 to > 65 %) in equatorial areas of East Africa in the NOAA CPC Week 2 Outlook for April 16th-22nd (Figure 4b). IRI’s longer range bi-weekly rainfall probability forecast for April 26th to May 9th, based on SubC models, indicates that above-normal rains could continue into early May (Figure 4c).

**Sea surface temperature changes and a weaker Pacific influence in eastern East Africa**

Between early February and mid-March to early April 2025, sea surface temperature (SST) conditions in the Indian Ocean substantially changed. SSTs that had been cooler than average in February and early March off the coast of the Eastern Horn and near the equator substantially warmed, and these SSTs are now average to above average (Figure 5). Above-average SSTs expanded across tropical areas. This has likely contributed to enhanced rainfall conditions by enhancing the moisture content of the low-level southerly and easterly winds (Figure 2).

La Niña conditions weakened during March and have since transitioned to neutral ENSO conditions, according to the Climate Prediction Center/NCEP/NWS[April 10th ENSO Diagnostic Discussion](https://www.cpc.ncep.noaa.gov/products/analysis_monitoring/enso_disc_apr2025/ensodisc.pdf). Weekly observations of Niño3.4 SST anomalies, as reported by the CPC, significantly changed between late February and early April. In late February the observations indicated quite cool conditions (~-0.8 °C). Current conditions are near average.

In the Pacific Ocean, Figure 5 shows the weakening of sea surface temperature anomalies during February and March 2025 in the western Pacific Warm Pool and the central-eastern equatorial Pacific. SSTs oscillated around near-average levels in March in the central-eastern equatorial Pacific (Niño3.4 region), and by the first week of April, previously cool Niño3.4 region SST (more centrally located) had also substantially warmed. Some atmospheric patterns continued to reflect La Niña conditions during March, such as low-level wind anomalies that were easterly over the western and central equatorial Pacific Ocean, consistent with the warm-to-cool SST gradient across these areas.

Coming into MAM 2025, expectations for a dry MAM season in eastern East Africa by the CHC, ICPAC and many modeling centers were based on climate model forecasts, La Niña conditions, and SST observations. SST observations indicated the presence of a strong negative Western V Gradient (an index which represents the tropical-subtropical Pacific basin SST gradient), and NMME seasonal forecasts, for example, had[indicated](https://www.cpc.ncep.noaa.gov/products/NMME/archive/2025010800/current/images/NMME_ensemble_tmpsfc_lead2.png)that a strong gradient could persist at least into March. The large ~0.8 °C increase in Niño3.4 Pacific SST led to a reduction in the strength of the Western V Gradient. Based on the April NMME SST forecast, Western V Gradient conditions will likely be moderately negative during May 2025. The central and eastern Pacific SST will likely remain near average, while the amplified warmth in ‘Western V’ region SSTs is forecast to continue.

**Conclusion**

The recent low-level wind anomalies from the south and southeast have been stronger than normal and influential, contributing to elevated moisture in eastern equatorial and southern East Africa. There is convergence among the recent GEFS/CHIRPS-GEFS, NOAA, and IRI/SubC rainfall forecasts for there being increased chances of above-average rainfall into mid April and possibly through early May.

Over the past several months, our blogs have relayed observed conditions, long lead climate forecasts, and outlooks for the MAM rainy season in eastern East Africa. Western Pacific sea surface temperatures in January and February were very high, concurrent La Niña conditions and model forecasts indicated a strong Western V Gradient, and observed cool Indian Ocean SSTs off the coast of Kenya and Somalia all indicated an elevated chance of below-normal MAM rains. Seasonal climate model forecasts showed elevated chances of below-normal rainfall leading up to the MAM 2025 season, including forecasts released in[February](https://www.cpc.ncep.noaa.gov/products/NMME/archive/2025020800/prob/images/prob_ensemble_prate_season1.png)and[March](https://www.cpc.ncep.noaa.gov/products/NMME/archive/2025030800/prob/images/prob_ensemble_prate_season1.png).

Previous concerns about a possible drying influence of a negative Western V Gradient during MAM 2025 in eastern Kenya, southern Somalia, and southeastern Ethiopia (and back-to-back poor rainfall seasons in southern Somalia) have greatly diminished, given rainfall performance through early April, the neutral ENSO, and the latest forecasts.
