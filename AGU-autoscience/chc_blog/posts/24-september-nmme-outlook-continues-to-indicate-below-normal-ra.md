# September NMME outlook continues to indicate below-normal rains for the eastern Horn

Chris Funk and Laura Harrison

**NMME SST Forecasts**

In early September, sea surface temperature (SST) and precipitation forecasts from seven North American Multi-Model Ensemble (NMME) models continue to suggest that below-normal rainfall outcomes for the eastern Horn (eHorn) are likely during October-November-December of 2024. Over the past several months, our blogs have relayed the latest forecasts and described how three SST indices can be used to anticipate OND eHorn rains (see CHC blogs in[July](https://blog.chc.ucsb.edu/?p=1418)and[August](https://blog.chc.ucsb.edu/?p=1444)). These indicators represent key oceanic forcing regions for eHorn precipitation, and are: 1) the Indian Ocean Dipole (IOD), 2) equatorial West Pacific SST and 3) Nino3.4 SST.

Figure 1 shows the latest (September) NMME ensemble mean forecast for OND 2024 SST anomalies across Indian and Pacific Ocean areas. Boxes in Figure 1 outline the indicator regions. SST anomalies are computed using the 1991-2020 hindcast mean. Overall, this map resembles SST anomalies during eHorn dry seasons (see Figure 3a in this[post](https://blog.chc.ucsb.edu/?p=1418)), with warm eastern Indian Ocean and West Pacific SST anomalies combined with cool equatorial eastern Pacific SST anomalies in the Nino3.4 region. The IOD and Nino3.4 are widely tracked indices, and numerous FEWS NET studies (most recent[here](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1029/2023EF003524)) have shown that the standardized difference between the Nino3.4 and West Pacific region – the West Pacific Gradient (WPG) – is a useful indicator of eHorn OND dry seasons.

The latest forecasts for all three indicators are more indicative of dry eHorn outcomes than last month. The chances of strong negative IOD or strong La Niña conditions developing during OND 2024 remain relatively low, and this yields a moderately dry eHorn rainfall outlook overall. That outlook is based on substantial changes in SST conditions during the next 1-2 months. As of early September, La Niña conditions have not yet developed, though the NMME models show generally strong agreement for Pacific SST forecasts.

The individual model forecasts for OND 2024 for the IOD, West Pacific, and Nino3.4 regions are shown in Table 1. The mean IOD forecast of -0.28°C,  the range of model IOD forecasts, and neutral early September IOD observations point to a weak negative IOD gradient during OND 2024. The anticipated WPG value for OND is much more negative (-1.7Z), associated with predictions for a very warm West Pacific (+0.63°C) and a moderate strength La Niña. The mean Nino3.4 region SST forecast is -1.1°C, and all models anticipate Nino3.4 SST anomalies below -0.5°C.

Figure 2 shows a scatter plot of observed WPG and IOD OND values. The color of the circles in this plot denotes below-normal, normal and above-normal eHorn rainy seasons, as determined by Standardized Precipitation Index (SPI) values. The green circle identifies the current 2024 WPG and IOD forecasts (-1.7Z, -0.28°C). The tan ellipse denotes the associated 80thpercentile confidence bounds associated with these NMME forecasts. There is a 90% chance that there will be a moderate or strongly negative WPG in OND of 2024 (WPG <-1Z). There is a 90% chance of the IOD being less than +0.1C. Historically, such a combination of conditions has been associated with very frequent dry seasons.

As discussed in our previous[post](https://blog.chc.ucsb.edu/?p=1418), the influence of the IOD, West Pacific, and Nino3.4 region  can be combined in a single indicator: the Indo-Warm Pool Heating Gradient (IWHG). According to this framework, a more negative IWHG indicates a greater amplification of the Indian Ocean branch of the Indo-Pacific Walker Circulation, and an increase in the  probability of dry eHorn conditions. The construction of IWHG index forecasts assigns coefficients to forecast SST values based upon historical regression analysis (see Table 1 “IWHG Slope” values for these coefficients). The current forecast for the OND 2024 IWHG is -252 Wm-2. This value indicates a moderately strong negative IWHG. The September OND 2024 IWHG is similar to the forecast made in July (-256 Wm-2). It is more negative than the August forecast (-189 Wm-2), due to the August to September shift in NMME models towards a slightly cooler Nino3.4 SST, a warmer West Pacific, and a neutral but more negative-leaning IOD value. The IOD can have a large impact on IWHG values due to its large coefficient. The September IWHG forecast is informed by (in order of highest to lowest overall influence) the West Pacific, Nino3.4, and IOD, due to differences in forecast values (Table 1, “Slope x NMME”).

Figure 3 shows a scatter plot of observed OND IWHG values and observed eHorn SPI.

The color of circles in this plot denotes below-normal, normal and above-normal eHorn rainy seasons. Also shown on this plot is the 2024 IWHG forecast of -252 Wm-2, and the associated eHorn SPI regression-based forecast (-0.9Z). A -0.9Z SPI value is associated with about a 20thpercentile or 1-in-5 year dry season. The tan ellipse denotes the associated 80thpercentile confidence bounds associated with these predictions. There is a 90% chance that there will be a moderate or strongly negative IWHG value (IWHG <-100 Wm-2). Historically, seasons in which IWHG values were less than -100 Wm-2frequently had below-normal eHorn rainfall.

IWHG regression-based rainfall forecasts for three scenarios are presented in Figure 4. This figure is the same as presented last month, but the middle panel, rather than the left panel, has become the most likely outcome.

**Selected Analogs**

Analog years for OND 2024 were identified using the IWHG forecast made in September 2024. A large number of years (14) represents a relatively wide range of possible IWHG conditions, under the current situation of a yet-to-develop La Niña event and currently neutral IOD conditions. Analogs were identified from years with observed OND IWHG values that were similar to the current forecast value and within 1.28 times the standard error of IWHG predictability, based on analysis of past forecasts and observations (IWHG values between -252 Wm-2+/- 145 Wm-2). This corresponds with the 80thpercentile confidence interval. Several years with strong La Niña conditions also fell within that range, but were not selected because a strong La Niña event is unlikely during OND 2024. The analogs based on the September 2024 IWHG forecast are: OND 1983, 1984, 1985, 1995, 1996, 2000, 2001, 2005, 2008, 2016, 2017, 2020, 2021, and 2022.

**NMME  and SubX Probability Forecasts**

We conclude by presenting NMME-based probabilities for very warm (> 80thpercentile) temperatures (Figure  5) and very low (< 20thpercentile) rainfall (Figure 6). These analyses take advantage of the 124 individual NMME simulations, and are available at[https://chc.ucsb.edu/monitoring/nmme/](https://chc.ucsb.edu/monitoring/nmme/).  Figure 5 shows that there is a high chance (>50%) of very warm temperatures. Such warm temperatures would exacerbate the impacts of below-normal rains, desiccating rangeland and reducing crops. Figure 6 suggests that there is a fairly high chance (~30-40%) of eastern Kenya, eastern Ethiopia, central Somalia and southern Somalia receiving extremely low rains. Sub-seasonal forecasts from the[IRI](https://iridl.ldeo.columbia.edu/maproom/Global/ForecastsS2S/precip_subx.html)are shown in Figure 7 for September 21stto October 4th.

**Conclusion**

While the lack of observed La Niña and IOD conditions persist, the latest NMME and IRI SubX models continue to anticipate La Niña-like conditions and below-normal rains for the eHorn region. As discussed in a recent multi-agency[alert](https://reliefweb.int/report/ethiopia/preparedness-planning-needed-potential-drought-eastern-horn-africa-during-october-december-rainy-season), there is broad consensus that “*Preparedness Planning is Needed for Potential Drought in the Eastern Horn of Africa during the October – December Rainy Season*.” The CHC’s latest forecast indicates a high probability of below-normal rains, with dry conditions likely to be exacerbated by above-normal air temperatures.
