# Consistent with the recent GHACOF69 outlook, a February forecast of a negative March-April-May 2025 Western V Gradient indicates that below-normal Gu/Long rains are likely

**Chris Funk and Laura Harrison**

To inform the latest outlooks for eastern East Africa’s March-April-May (MAM) 2025 rainfall season, this brief post presents the February model forecast of the MAM 2025 ‘Western V Gradient’ (WVG). Negative WVG values indicate an elevated chance of below-normal MAM eastern East African rains ([2023 paper 1](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1029/2023EF003524)), due to an intensification ([2023 paper 2](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1029/2022EF003454)) of the Indian Ocean branch of the Walker Circulation (a large-scale circulation that controls the east and westward movement of air at different atmospheric levels).

The Western V Gradient measures the west-versus-east sea surface temperature (SST) gradient across the Pacific, which influences and is influenced by the Walker Circulation. During La Niña-like climate conditions, above the equatorial Pacific, stronger than normal trade winds blow from the eastern Pacific into the air above Indonesia. This produces moisture convergence and heavy rains. To the west of this precipitation, over eastern East Africa, air sinks, creating hotter conditions, and elevated chances of drought. The WVG provides a convenient way of describing the Pacific SST gradients that enhance the Walker Circulation.

The WVG index is based on the difference in standardized sea surface temperature anomalies (SST) in the Nino3.4 and Western V regions (shown in Fig. 1 below). Current[observed](https://www.cpc.ncep.noaa.gov/products/precip/CWlink/MJO/enso.shtml#forecast)Nino3.4 SST conditions are quite cool, with a weekly anomaly of  -0.8°C. Both in the observed climate and the 2025 MAM predictions, these cool east Pacific SST conditions lie alongside a very warm ‘Western V’ region. Beginning in the area near Indonesia, and stretching to the northeast and southwest, the models anticipate very warm sea surface temperature conditions. The interaction of these anomalies is expected to increase precipitation near Indonesia, and decrease precipitation over eastern East Africa.

The NMME MAM 2025 precipitation forecasts, updated in February, also indicate suppressed rainfall over eastern East Africa (Fig. 2). These results are consistent with the recent forecast[issued](https://www.icpac.net/publications/statement-from-the-69th-greater-horn-of-africa-climate-outlook-forum-ghacof69/)by the 69th Greater Horn of Africa Climate Outlook Forum.

Consistent with[research](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41612-019-0091-7)suggesting a trend towards shorter MAM rainy seasons, the MAM forecast anticipates very warm SST conditions off the southeast coast of Africa and along the northern edge of the Indian Ocean. Such SST may cause a delayed onset, as March rains linger longer in Mozambique and Madagascar, and then a more rapid cessation in May, as very warm SST in the the northern Indian Ocean bring an earlier onset of boreal summer Indian monsoon. Another interesting feature of the forecast is a very warm equatorial eastern Indian Ocean SST. These very warm conditions are similar to a negative Indian Ocean Dipole-like response, which has appeared during or after many recent La Niña events (See Fig. 8[here](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1029/2022EF003454)). Interestingly, NMME MAM 2025 precipitation forecasts indicate rainfall increases in all three of these regions, along with suppressed rainfall over eastern East Africa.

To put the current Western V Gradient forecast in context, Fig. 3 displays a scatter plot of observed and predicted MAM WVG values. The February NMME forecasts of MAM WVG are extremely skillful, with a historicalR2of 0.93. The 2025 WVG forecast is -1.4 Z, with an 80th percentile confidence interval of ∓0.3 Z. This implies that we are almost certain to see a strong negative WVG in MAM 2025. Such conditions are very likely, even if Nino3.4 SST warm beyond the standard La Niña threshold of -0.5 °C. Consistent with prior CHC outlooks, we can select prior seasons with similar negative WVG values: 1989, 1999, 2000, 2008, 2011, 2012, 2018, 2021, 2022. In eastern East Africa, six of these nine seasons had below-normal MAM rains, while 2012 was a normal season, 1989 was slightly above average, and 2018 was exceptionally wet.MJO events can also influence seasonal rains, such as in 2018, but are not predicted well this far in advance.At a regional scale (i.e. based on average rainfall over eastern East Africa), these results suggest a ~66% chance of below-normal MAM 2025 rains.

Poor eastern East African MAM rains in Somalia (i.e.*Gu*rains) might be most immediately hazardous in Somalia, where the prior October-November-December 2024 season was associated with moderate or severe drought, especially in the south-central region of the country (Fig. 4).

![Figure 4. FAO SWALIM Compound Drought Indicator for October-November-December 2024. Map obtained from https://cdi.faoswalim.org/index/cdi .](https://blog.chc.ucsb.edu/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/SWALIM_CDI.png)

In the past, consecutive dry seasons in Somalia have been associated with severe impacts on herds and pastoralists, as well as high levels of food insecurity. Current vegetation[conditions](https://earlywarning.usgs.gov/fews/product/900)over most of eastern East Africa are poor, and poor rangeland health could be exacerbated by a poor MAM rainy season, combined with forecasts for[abnormally high temperatures](https://data.chc.ucsb.edu/products/NMME/Archive/202502/E_Africa/plot_E_Africa_3month_tref_pctl_moderate_events_forecast_probability_IC_month_202502.png).
