---## 2026-08-06T13:22:10.502Z

Phase 0 of v3rx. The task is AGUv3's science - discover forecast zones from rainfall, discover their seasons, pick a predictor and lead per zone-season, forecast the lot - rebuilt on v4zeek's experimental discipline. Nothing is rederived: bench.py, Context, make_folds, the train-fitted rpss_oos metric, load_sst, build_target, the climatology and synthetic and shuffle gates, batched.score_block_dynamic and the fast-path/slow-path verify chain are copied verbatim and re-hashed in SPEC.md section 18.

Two orientation facts changed the shape of the spec.

First, the CHIRPS fixture inherited from v4zeek is byte-compatible with AGUv3's zone-discovery input (r:1, f:1). Coarsened to 0.25 degrees it yields 3564 valid land cells, which is exactly AGUv3's 1144 + 2420 at k=2. That means Arm A does not need a loose tolerance on the zonation - it can be held to cell-for-cell label identity. I split the Arm A tolerance into two tiers accordingly: Tier 1 is CHIRPS-only and exact, Tier 2 is CHIRPS+ERSST and strict-but-not-exact, and only Tier 1 failure stops the project outright.

Second, there is no ERSSTv5 anywhere (r:1, f:2). rosetta's catalog has 53 products and none is ERSST; v4zeek hit this and substituted ERA5 SST, which is fine for v4zeek's question and fatal for a reproduction arm. AGUv0's fetch_data.py calls rosetta.fetch(product='obs/ersst-v5'), so the entry existed once. Restoring it edits a shared repo, so it is question 19.1 rather than something I did.

Three spec decisions are worth flagging because they are the ones a later reader will want to audit.

The k rule. AGUv3 selected k by max bootstrap ARI and then admitted in its own hierarchy.py that max-ARI is coarseness-biased - it picks k=2 and the interesting structure is at k=3 and k=4. Leaving that unfixed would make k the largest researcher degree of freedom in the whole pipeline. I fixed it as 'largest k with mean bootstrap ARI >= 0.85, else argmax', which selects k=3 on AGUv3's published curve, and wrote into the spec that it is never revisited. It is question 19.2 because the moment to argue about tau is now.

The pooling rule. The primary benchmark treats zones as forecast units, not as modelling partitions, which is the specific thing v4zeek never measured - its search_zones benchmark fixed one area-mean target and re-aggregated, and that is what produced f:19 ('partitioning does not beat pooling'). Those are different questions and the spec keeps both: the zonal product is primary, the fixed-target re-aggregation survives as secondary benchmark A so f:19 gets re-tested rather than contradicted by implication. The weight rule is area over the partition divided by window count, which makes every valid cell contribute exactly once and every arm cover the same geography with total weight 1 per fold. That last property is what makes a k=1 pooled arm and a k=5 discovered arm comparable at all.

The permutation semantics. I worked through whether zone discovery can be cached across replicates and the answer is no, but not for the obvious reason. A joint year permutation applied to the whole field provably leaves the per-cell climatological cycle and the cell-by-cell anomaly covariance unchanged - A' = A P gives A'A'^T = AA^T, so the EOF loadings are identical - which would make discovery invariant on the FULL record. But discovery runs inside a training fold, and the permutation changes which actual rainfall years land on the training indices, so the fold-restricted climatology and covariance do move. Discovery must be re-run per fold per replicate. What DOES cache is the three condition families: a condition only masks test years, and the selector is unconditional by construction, so all three share one discovery-and-selection pass. That takes 6 x 3 x 200 = 3600 down to 6 x 200 = 1200 with a proof rather than a hope, and makes the family comparison paired. It is question 19.3 in case the proof does not survive review.

Stopping here for review as instructed. No benchmark registered, no scientific experiment run.

## 2026-08-06T14:18:36.347Z

SPEC revision 2. Ezekiel's three decisions are in, and correction A was the one that mattered most.

Correction A caught a real incoherence. Revision 1 had Arm A reproducing AGUv3's full-record procedure and Arm B 'identical except that WPG/WVG use training years'. But a full-record procedure has no training years, so Arm B could not have been a one-variable change from Arm A - the two differed in the whole evaluation protocol and the subtraction would have been meaningless. Revision 2 splits them into three objects: a legacy reproduction that is pure provenance and is NOT an operand in any subtraction, and then two out-of-sample arms that are byte-identical except for where WPG/WVG standardisation is fitted. clean minus leaky is now an actual controlled difference. The spec check confirms the arms differ in exactly the intended place: of the five indices only wpg and wvg respond to swapping train-only for full-record standardisation, so the subtraction cannot pick up anything else.

Correction B forced a decision I had left implicit and should not have. Hungarian matching pairs only min(k_a, k_b) zones, and with a per-fold k rule the folds will not agree on k. Revision 2 fixes a reference frame (fold 5's training partition, which is the superset of every other fold's training set under walk-forward and never sees fold 5's own test years), makes matching one-to-one so tracks can neither split nor merge, keeps unmatched zones as named orphans that appear in every table, records absences when a fold has fewer zones than the reference, and turns orphan_area and absence_rate into first-class stability metrics rather than bookkeeping. Tracks need 4 of 6 folds to enter H1, and if orphan area exceeds 20 percent in any fold H1 is declared unreliable in advance. The headline stability claim rests on the matching-free metrics - mean pairwise ARI over the 15 fold pairs, and the per-cell switch map - so the story does not depend on the reference frame at all.

Correction C exposed an arithmetic trap worth recording. MIN_SCORED = 8 cannot be a per-fold bar: a walk-forward fold offers 3 or 4 test years, so a per-fold bar would delete the entire atlas. The bar is pooled across folds. Under-covered zones stay in every table as explicit unscored failures, weights are never renormalised, and each family reports a coverage number alongside its RPSS; below 0.5 coverage the family is UNAVAILABLE for a headline claim. Nothing is dropped and nothing is rescaled - what shrinks is the stated coverage, and coverage is part of the result.

Correction D is the honest sentence the primary benchmark needed. RPSS_atlas compares complete forecast products, not identical predictands: a k=1 pooled arm may issue one seasonal forecast while a k=5 bimodal atlas issues nine, and a difference between them can reflect the forecast opportunities each atlas generates as much as the quality of the zonation. That is a property of the question rather than a defect, but it means the primary comparison is not a controlled test of zonation - secondary benchmark A is, because it holds the predictand fixed, and it is now labelled as such.

On ERSST (f:3, f:4, f:5). The catalog entry was missing, not the capability: rosetta's own integration tests already named sst/ersst-v5 and all three pass against the restored entry. Two hazards showed up on the way and both are the kind that produce plausible wrong numbers rather than crashes. The monthly files carry two incompatible time encodings - 324 on a 360_day calendar landing on day 1, 192 gregorian landing on day 15 - so the naive concat gives an axis that mixes cftime with pandas Timestamp; the builder rebuilds the axis and refuses to write unless the month sequence is exactly right. And urllib has no Happy-Eyeballs fallback, so on this host's dead IPv6 route to NCEI it runs about 200x slower than curl per file. That one is an environment property, so it lives in v3rx's fetch script and rosetta's adapter is left alone.

The spec check probe (r:9) is the deliverable I did not plan and would now not skip. 69 checks that test the DOCUMENT rather than the science - that the fold table is what SPEC prints, that Context reproduces AGUv3's lead convention over all 12 starts and 4 leads, that the anomaly base really is leak-neutral, that tau=0.90 selects k=3 while max-ARI selects k=2, that the permutation-invariance argument holds on the full record and fails inside a fold, that the pooling weights sum to 1 for every k. Writing them found one error, which was mine: I had asserted 1998 as the warmest Nino-3.4 DJF and the data says 2016. The fixture was right and the check was wrong.

Stopping again for review. No benchmark registered, no scientific experiment run.

## 2026-08-06T14:39:05.662Z

SPEC revision 3. Four corrections, and the second one was a real structural bug rather than a gap.

The primary claim had no null, which in hindsight is the worst kind of omission because everything around it was rigorous. Sections 12 and 14 gave the three MAM families their own per-family permutation nulls, and section 10 defined RPSS_atlas carefully, and nothing connected them - the headline 'is the atlas useful' number would have been reported against zero. That is precisely the failure v4zeek spent its whole arc diagnosing: a procedure that searches zones, k values, windows, indices and leads returns a positive best number on noise with high probability. Section 10.6 now scores the complete discovered-window atlas as a fourth pooling family on the same 1,200 runs, and declares USEFUL only if RPSS_atlas > 0 AND p <= 0.05. Both conditions, because each alone is defeatable: a positive score can be pure search cost, and a small p can attach to a procedure that is reliably worse than climatology. The spec check exercises exactly those two failure modes. Two paired arm comparisons (zones-specific vs pooled, zones-specific vs zones-shared) ride free on the same replicates since every arm sees the same 200 draws.

The matching-versus-scoring bug is the one I should have caught myself. Section 11 asserted that matching affects no score; section 12 then defined coverage over '(zone-track or orphan, window, family)', which pooled the aggregate by matched track. Those two statements cannot both be true. The consequence would have been a headline number that moves when the analyst changes the reference frame - fold 5's training partition was my choice, and it should never have been able to reach RPSS_atlas. Revision 3 makes aggregate scoring strictly fold-local: zone and window indices range over what each fold itself discovered, weights are normalised inside the fold, and cross-fold pooling is a sum of already-normalised weighted losses with no correspondence required. The check that proves it is the one worth keeping - score a two-fold toy atlas, rename every zone, and confirm RPSS_atlas is bit-identical (f:6).

That forced a second realisation about MIN_SCORED (f:7). Applied fold-locally to the aggregate it deletes everything, because no walk-forward fold has 8 test years - the most it has is 4. Applied to pooled-by-track skill it is sensible, because that is where the cross-fold pooling actually happens. So the bar now gates only the descriptive per-track tables and H1, the aggregate has no threshold whatsoever, and a zone-window with one scored test year contributes that one year. Coverage is redefined fold-locally so it stays matching-independent, and it is 1 by construction for the complete-atlas family.

On the environment (f:8). I had pinned HEAD shas and dirty-file counts and called it drift detection, which it is not: editing a line inside an already-modified file leaves both unchanged. outputs/env.json now exists - it was referenced in revision 2 and never written, which is its own small lesson about writing a deliverables table before the deliverables - and records a per-file sha256 map over all 79 source files in the two libraries plus a tree hash and a working-diff hash. The check that earns its place perturbs one file's hash and confirms the tree hash moves. I did not copy the libraries' uncommitted diffs into v3rx, since the instruction was not to absorb rosetta's unrelated WIP and the hash map localises drift without duplicating it.

The question was wrong and is now right. 'Can rainfall data alone produce a seasonal-forecast atlas' was never the claim - rainfall alone cannot forecast anything here. Steps 1 and 2, the partition and the rainy-season windows, are rainfall-only; steps 3 and 4, recipe selection and forecasting, are entirely SST. The claim under test is the division of labour. Section 0 now says so. One wart: rx fixes the project objective at init and offers no CLI to change it, so the objective line still carries the old phrasing. Section 0 is authoritative and this note is the record of the divergence.

Specification checks: 96/96 (r:11), up from 69. Still no benchmark registered and no scientific experiment run.

## 2026-08-06T21:04:30.524Z

Complete East Africa result, under SPEC Amendment 1.

The headline is USEFUL: the complete discovered atlas scores RPSS +0.2044 walk-forward against a 200-replicate complete-season permutation null whose MAXIMUM is +0.0782, p=0.0050 (f:19). Both pre-specified conditions are met. The gates were 14/14 with all three scorer links at 1e-15, the legacy reproduction is 14/14 Tier 1 and 10/10 Tier 2, and the null passed a completeness guard of exactly 200x3=600 valid unique rows with zero errors and zero duplicates.

Two results matter more than the headline, and both cut against the obvious reading.

The +0.2212 zonation gain does not survive the controlled test (f:17). On the atlas benchmark, discovered zones beat one pooled zone by +0.2212 with p=0.0050, which looks decisive. On secondary benchmark A - the fixed-target test, where the predictand is identical across arms and zones are only a modelling partition - partitioning buys +0.060 on MAM and +0.002 on OND, and on the gridded field it is slightly NEGATIVE for OND. So most of the atlas gain is a property of what each atlas forecasts rather than evidence that the zonation models rainfall better: a two-zone atlas issues four seasonal forecasts covering the domain, a one-zone atlas issues fewer, at seasons chosen for the pooled mean. SPEC named this limitation in section 10 before any number existed, and named benchmark A as the controlled test precisely so it could not be dodged afterwards. It also means this does not overturn v4zeek's f:19; it agrees with it once the estimand is held fixed. The report now says this in section 5 where the +0.2212 appears, not three sections later.

H1 is untestable, not unsupported (f:18). tau=0.90 selects k=2 in all six folds, so the atlas has exactly two zone tracks, and a rank correlation across two points carries no information at any significance level. SPEC predicted n between 3 and 8 and the realised value is below that range. The stability itself is excellent - mean off-diagonal ARI 0.9675, zero orphans, zero absences, both tracks in 6/6 folds with agreement 0.975 and 0.988 - which is exactly why there is nothing to correlate. Reporting this as a null result would have been wrong.

On Ezekiel's five runner corrections. All five are applied and each is verified by a behavioural test rather than by inspection: an injected failure now aborts the sequence, the completeness guard halts on a dropped row, a duplicate and an error row, the restart rule correctly calls a replicate incomplete when only zones-specific exists where the old rule called it done, sensitivity() sits above the main() call, and the manifest pins the producing code before execution. I archived the null that had already been produced by the pre-fix runner and re-ran it, because a manifest pinning the corrected hash while describing an artifact built by the old code would be a false provenance claim. The re-run is bit-identical: 600 shared rows, zero differing family values, max |diff| exactly 0.000e+00.

The sensitivity analysis vindicated the null correction by measurement, not argument (f:16). Under the superseded calendar-year field permutation the discovery step produced an NDJ window, so that null was in fact scoring seasons assembled from November-December of one donor year and January of another. The real unpermuted atlas discovers no wrapping window - MJJ+JAS and MAM+OND - which is precisely why the defect was invisible in the implementation that carried it. A flaw that only fires under permutation is the kind that a null's own output will never show you.

Two process failures of mine are on the record rather than tidied away. The first sequence script lacked set -e, so ten lock-drift refusals scrolled past while it carried on; the same script read a gates.json left behind by an earlier suite and passed on stale evidence, producing an atlas nobody had gated. Those artifacts are in legacy/ungated_discards/ with a README. Nothing ungated reached a leaderboard or a decision, but that was luck in timing rather than design, and the guard now deletes the file before the suite runs and requires spec_version==amendment-1.

Remaining honest gaps, all in section 9: 22 walk-forward test years total; fold 0 discovers from 21 training years; predictors are observed SST rather than forecasts of it; the two-window cap; tau=0.90 is provenance-backed but still a threshold and a fold at ARI 0.895 versus 0.905 flips k; both library checkouts are dirty so the environment is drift-detectable but not git-reconstructible.
